Kimi K3 Architecture Design from SGLang

Source of truth: sgl-project/sglang branch kimi-k3 @ 36e31ee (2026-08-04). The model is not on main — only python/sglang/kernels/ops/kimi_k3/ is. Companion markdown: README.md, 01–05 in this folder.

0 · What this model is, in plain English

The 30-second version. Kimi K3 is a large multimodal reasoning model. Its defining trick is a hybrid memory design: about three-quarters of its layers keep a small fixed-size running summary of the conversation instead of a full transcript, so long contexts stay cheap, while the remaining quarter does exact lookback to keep quality high. Its thinking compute is a mixture of 896 experts of which only 16 run per word, and it compresses the data in half before shipping it to those experts to halve the network cost. It also keeps a small "scrapbook" of earlier internal states that every layer can blend from — an unusual feature most models don't have. SGLang is the engine that serves it, and most of the engineering there is about fusing operations together so data gets moved once instead of several times.

Kimi K3 is a large language model from Moonshot AI — the same category of thing as GPT, Claude or DeepSeek. You give it text or images, it produces text. SGLang is the serving engine: the software that actually runs the model on GPUs fast enough to be useful. The model is the recipe; SGLang is the kitchen. Same recipe, a good kitchen serves a hundred covers an hour instead of five.

The one thing to understand: it's a stack of 93 layers

Text comes in at the bottom, passes through 93 near-identical stages, and a prediction comes out the top. Each stage does two jobs — attention ("which earlier words matter for the word I'm writing now?", which is how the model knows what "it" refers to three paragraphs back) and a feed-forward step (the actual thinking about the word, once attention has gathered the context). Nearly every modern LLM is that pattern. What makes K3 distinctive is how it does each of the two, plus one genuinely unusual thing about how information flows between layers.

What the architecture contains

① Two kinds of attention, mixed together — 69 + 24

Standard attention has an expensive habit: to answer about word 100,000 it re-examines a stored record of all 99,999 earlier words. That record — the KV cache — grows without limit as the conversation lengthens. This is the central cost problem in serving LLMs. K3 splits the difference:

69 layers — KDA, the "running notes"
Recurrent. Instead of a record of every past word, each layer keeps a fixed-size summary it updates as it reads — notes rather than a full transcript. Same size whether the conversation is 1,000 words or a million. Cheap and constant, but lossy.

The "delta" trick: when a new word arrives the layer doesn't blindly append it. It checks what the notes already predict about that word and writes down only the part it got wrong. Correcting your notes rather than re-copying the page.
24 layers — MLA, the "full transcript"
Exact. Real attention that does look back at everything — but stores each past word compressed rather than at full size. This is the design DeepSeek popularised; K3 borrows it essentially unchanged.

Precise but expensive: these are the only layers whose memory grows with conversation length.

Why mix them? Recurrent layers are cheap but forgetful; exact layers are precise but expensive. Roughly three-quarters cheap and one-quarter exact keeps quality up while making long contexts affordable — only 24 of 93 layers pay the growing-memory cost. The practical consequence: K3's long-context footprint is dominated by a fixed per-user allocation rather than a per-word one, and operators tune the balance between the two pools with one knob, --mamba-full-memory-ratio.

② A mixture of experts — 896 specialists, 17 consulted per word

The feed-forward half of each layer isn't one big block — it's 896 smaller ones ("experts") plus one generalist that always participates. For each individual word a small router picks the 16 most relevant experts, and only those run. The other 880 sit idle. That's why the model can be enormous in total size yet fast in practice: a large panel of consultants where you only ever call in the handful you need, and different words wake up different experts.

K3 adds an unusual wrinkle. Before consulting the experts it shrinks the data to half size (7168 numbers per word → 3584), runs the experts in that compressed space, then expands back. Since the experts live on different GPUs, the data must be shipped across the network to reach them — and shrinking it first halves the network traffic. In a big deployment that network hop is often the slowest step in the entire layer.

③ The attention-residual bank — K3's most unusual feature

In an ordinary model, information passes from layer 1 to 2 to 3 in a single stream, each layer adding its contribution. Later layers see only the accumulated total; the intermediate states are gone.

K3 keeps a scrapbook. Every few layers it saves a snapshot of the current state (up to 8 snapshots). Then at each layer, instead of just reading the latest state, it looks at all the saved snapshots plus the current one and learns how much weight to give each. A layer can decide "for this word, the state as of layer 20 is more useful than the state right now" and lean on it. Version history with the ability to blend versions, rather than a single overwritten document.

This is the feature with no equivalent in DeepSeek-V3 or most other open models — and it's what the largest share of the custom kernel work in this SGLang branch exists to make fast.

④ No position numbers in the exact-attention layers

Most models explicitly tag each word with its position ("this is word 47"). K3's 24 MLA layers don't do this at all. Position information reaches them implicitly via the 69 recurrent layers, which read strictly in order and so inherently know where they are. A quirk with real consequences for engineers: a lot of highly-optimised attention code assumes those position tags exist and fuses their computation into the main kernel. For K3 there is nothing to fuse.

⑤ Vision

Images go through a separate vision tower that converts a picture into the same kind of numbers the text layers consume, after which the model treats image and text content uniformly. K3 handles transparent images by compositing them onto a configurable background rather than mangling the transparency — small, but a real improvement over the previous generation.

What SGLang does to make it fast

If you remember one idea from the engineering side, make it this one: at the scale these models run, the bottleneck is usually moving data, not computing on it. GPUs finish the arithmetic and then wait — for memory, or for the network between GPUs. So nearly all the optimisation work in this branch is a variation on a single theme: stop moving things twice.
TechniqueThe plain version
Kernel fusionTwo operations that ran back-to-back get merged into one, so the intermediate result never leaves the chip. K3 does this a dozen different ways — even fusing a network operation with the math that follows it.
Merged weightsThree separate small multiplications that all read the same input become one bigger multiplication. Read the input once instead of three times.
OverlappingWhile the GPU waits on the network, give it unrelated math to do. K3 runs the generalist expert during the network wait for the specialists — worth about 4–5% throughput, essentially for free.
Splitting across GPUsSeveral different axes: split the layers, the experts, the attention heads, or the words. Each has different traffic costs, and the right combination depends on the hardware.
Most of this only runs on NVIDIA Blackwell (B200/B300/GB200/GB300), which has capabilities older generations lack. On NVIDIA Hopper (H100/H200) and on AMD the model still runs correctly — it just falls back to generic, slower paths for these specific optimisations.

Why latent MoE matters operationally

The a2a payload is halved (3584 vs 7168 per token) — which is exactly why the DeepEP patch must add case 3584 to SWITCH_HIDDEN; stock DeepEP has no template instantiation for that width. It also means the down/up projections are ReplicatedLinear, so the routed output is already fully reduced when it leaves routed_expert_up_proj.

SiTU — activation.py:184-212

gate = beta * tanh(gate/beta) * sigmoid(gate)   # beta = 4.0
up   = linear_beta * tanh(up/linear_beta)       # 25.0, soft clip
out  = gate * up

The constants are asserted, not read on the MegaMoE path (kimi_k3.py:465-471) — DeepGEMM bakes them in, so a checkpoint with different betas is rejected at init rather than silently mis-activated. MegaMoE selects SiTU by a sentinel activation_clamp = 2^-5.

SP-MoE — sequence-parallel MoE region — kimi_k3.py:1904-1926

When an EP a2a backend is active and attn_tp > 1, the layer flips o_proj.reduce_results off and completes the attention reduction as a reduce-scatter. The entire MoE region — aggregation 2, norms, gate, latent projections, tp1 shared experts, a2a dispatch — then runs on 1/attn_tp of the rows, all-gathered back after the tail add.

The accounting: RS + AG moves the same bytes the o_proj all-reduce did; the shared-expert all-reduce disappears via tp1 weights; each rank dispatches only its shard, killing the attn_tp-fold dispatch redundancy. Net: strictly less communication, and the MoE front compute divided by attn_tp. Dense layers are excluded — a column-parallel MLP has no per-token decomposition that survives a token shard.

The recurrence

q,k,v = short_conv(q,k,v)          # 4-tap causal depthwise
beta  = sigmoid(b)                 # per-head, (0,1)
decay = exp(-softplus(f+dt_bias) * exp(A_log))
                                   # lower-bounded at -5.0
S = decay * S                      # gated forget
S = S + beta * (v - S @ k) k^T     # delta correction
o = S @ q
o = FusedRMSNormGated(o, g)        # o * rms(o) * sigmoid(g)
out = o_proj(o)

The delta term (v − S k) writes only the part of v the state does not already predict — that is what gives the family its associative-recall behaviour.

Attempt-and-verify fusion protocol (kimi_k3.py:1669-1694) — the module stashes the gate on the attention object (attn._k3_onorm_gate) and lets the kernel fold FusedRMSNormGated into the recurrence epilogue. DSPARK — speculative decoding on a recurrent model

kda_decode_mtp.py (~48 KB CuTe DSL) implements fused_kda_decode_mtp_dspark: dense contract T = num_requests × (1 + num_spec) (--speculative-dspark-block-size, default 7); optional ReplaySSM ring buffers (ring_rawv/rawk/g/beta) so the accepted prefix can be exactly re-folded into the state at commit time (--enable-linear-replayssm-spec); phase 1 conv precompute, phase 2 state tile loop in streaming or resident mode.

Recurrent state + speculation is the hard part of a hybrid model — you cannot just discard rejected KV rows, you have to roll the SSM state back. ReplaySSM is that mechanism.

5 · MLA layers — kimi_k3.py:1703-1873

KimiK3MLAAttention(DeepseekV2AttentionMLA) reuses DSV2/V3 MLA wholesale — q_lora_rank, kv_lora_rank, qk_nope/rope_head_dim, v_head_dim, MQA radix attention over the latent, absorb paths, DCP — and changes exactly two things.

① NoPE

super().__init__(..., skip_rope=True, ...)   # 1730

skip_rope=True sets self.rotary_emb = None (deepseek_v2.py:1882-1896). The MLA layers apply no rotary embedding. The 64 qk_rope_head_dim channels still exist and are still cached (KV cost is (512+64)/token/layer) but carry no positional rotation. Positional information comes from the KDA layers' causal conv + decay. Exposed as mla_use_nope.

For anyone porting attention kernels: the MLA layers are position-agnostic, so RoPE-fused MLA kernels have nothing to fuse.

② Output gate

x = x * sigmoid(g_proj(hidden_states))

g_proj: [H → num_heads*v_head_dim] sharded on attn_tp. Implemented by wrapping o_proj.forward at the instance level (1810-1836) because o_proj is called from deep inside the inherited MLA forward cores. The fused kernel mla_output_gate.py is documented to match the unfused pair bit-for-bit (double rounding preserved); covered(x, gate) gates its use.

The gate GEMM rides an alt stream (_precompute_output_gate, 1838-1858) — but only under CUDA-graph capture, only outside a breakable graph, and only to 128 tokens on Blackwell / 64 elsewhere.

Attention-TP correctness. K3 has no LayerCommunicator, so o_proj must reduce within the attention-TP group itself (use_dp_attention_reduce = True, kimi_k3.py:1771-1776, and 1474-1484 for KDA). The default full-TP collective would be the wrong group at attn_tp > 1 and would deadlock against idle DP ranks. Worth knowing if you are debugging a hang in a DP-attention K3 deployment.

Multimodal specifics

GPU preprocessing (KimiK3GPUProcessorWrapper, extends the K2.5 wrapper) preserves the alpha channel through bicubic resize and composites onto a background configured in the checkpoint's preprocessor_config.json (transparent_bg_config: chessboard / white / black / gray) — new in K3 relative to K2.5. One MultimodalDataItem must carry exactly one grid so CUDA-IPC lease accounting stays per-item.
